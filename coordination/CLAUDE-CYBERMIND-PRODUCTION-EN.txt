## Art direction and three-dimensional detail

Design a consistent visual language: authored silhouettes, a controlled palette, differentiated materials, motivated light sources, deliberate atmosphere, readable interfaces and a convincing sense of scale. Darkness and bloom must not erase geometry or conceal weak materials. Balance luminous accents with surfaces that can still be read in shadow and highlight.

Build true spatial depth: foreground, navigable subject space, midground structures and a complete background. Camera movement should reveal parallax, changing occlusion, reflections, internal structure and details that could not be communicated by a flat poster. Cinematic depth of field is an artistic option, not a substitute for dimensional construction; preserve a readable focus hierarchy and crisp detail where the viewer is looking.

Every allowed camera path and close viewing distance must be supported by the assets it exposes. Give hero objects credible layered construction and fine detail: surface transitions, edge treatment, joints, microstructure, moving parts or internal layers as appropriate to your design. Use fine geometry where silhouette, proximity or interaction requires it; use shaders, normal maps and instanced detail where they are effective. Prevent obvious intersections, floating joints, visible scene boundaries, stretched textures and empty reverse sides along permitted views.

Include living, purposeful motion at multiple scales, with a clear hierarchy. Motion should communicate the invented system's state or function, rather than merely adding unrelated rotation. Small interactions should reveal more about the world. Fictional data and simulated cognition must be identifiable as designed content; do not present decorative signals as real live intelligence or telemetry.

## Interaction and website experience

This must be an actual usable 3D website. Build one coherent interaction loop with several tightly connected interactions; the exact mechanism is your creative decision. Mouse, touch where feasible and basic keyboard controls should have clear feedback, selection/hover states, transitions and an understandable reset or return path.

At least one signature interaction must visibly and meaningfully transform the three-dimensional subject or environment. A second layer should reward closer exploration or inspection. The viewer should be able to discover the idea through use. Support repeat interaction without getting stuck in an animation or losing navigation. If scripted camera tours are offered, let the visitor interrupt them and regain control.

Use a restrained, responsive interface integrated with the visual world. Include only copy and panels that help visitors explore or understand the premise. Avoid a generic marketing page, repeated feature cards or a long tool tutorial around a small 3D widget. Loading, failure and unsupported-renderer states should remain usable. A cinematic tour may demonstrate the same underlying interactive scene, but prerecorded video must not substitute for functional interaction.

## 4K and 120fps: two separate acceptance targets

The user's requested quality target is native 3840x2160 and 120 frames per second. Preserve that ambition in both the website architecture and the demonstration pipeline. Do not quietly downgrade it and continue describing the result as 4K120.

A. Interactive website: target native-4K rendering at 120fps on a documented capable system. The corresponding frame budget is about 8.33 ms. Benchmark representative movement and interactions with the page visible and focused after warm-up. Record physical render-buffer dimensions, device pixel ratio, quality mode, browser, renderer/backend, hardware and display refresh when reliably available, average FPS and frame-time distribution. A 60Hz display or browser scheduling cap cannot demonstrate 120 presented frames per second, even if an offline render can generate them. If that is the available test environment, report the verification limit and optimize what can actually be measured. Do not fabricate a 4K120 real-time pass.

Provide explicitly labeled quality/performance profiles, including a native-4K fidelity profile and useful responsive fallbacks. Keep the flagship composition and interaction design intact across them. Dynamic resolution or lower-quality modes must be labeled honestly; do not count a low-resolution offscreen target stretched onto a 4K canvas as native-4K rendering. Profile the expensive passes, draw calls, shader/geometry load and texture/render-target memory before making targeted optimizations. Use instancing, batching, LOD, culling, streaming, selective high-resolution assets and appropriate lighting strategies as needed. Do not assume more geometry or an experimental rendering backend automatically means better quality.

B. Demonstration master: produce a genuine native 3840x2160 / 120fps video from the actual three-dimensional product. Use deterministic animation stepping at 1/120 second and render each output frame from that state. Verify the actual drawing-buffer/input dimensions throughout export, and retain representative native-resolution frames. No upscaling, motion interpolation or repeated 60fps frames labeled as 120fps. Choose a supported high-quality codec/container after a short encode/decode test. Target roughly 45-60 seconds with an intentional opening, signature transformation, close exploration and ending; adjust only if the concept genuinely needs it. A separate broadly compatible 60fps preview may be derived from the master and labeled as a derivative, not a replacement for the requested master.

Test a short representative native-4K120 segment BEFORE committing to the full export. Confirm actual frame count, duration, cadence, codec, sharpness, UI readability and motion. Avoid multiple full-length recordings for problems that a short test would reveal. A master rendered slower than real time is valid offline output, but does not prove a 120fps live website. If a required target is blocked by actual hardware, browser or encoder limits, report the exact unmet target with evidence and preserve the highest-quality valid outputs; do not relabel them as having passed.

## Production sequence and acceptance gates

1. Recover environment and requirements. Verify local share, tools, hardware and input references. Independently choose one strong creative direction; a brief comparison of up to three options is enough. Record a one-screen concept and scope. Proceed without waiting for routine design approval.
2. Prove the signature. Build a small real-3D vertical slice that shows the intended visual identity, one meaningful interaction, representative near-camera detail and a short camera move. Resolve concrete problems before scaling it into the whole site.
3. Establish the performance/export route. Measure the representative slice, choose the renderer and quality structure, and validate a short native-4K120 capture/encode. Record constraints early, while retaining the final quality target.
4. Complete the world and interactions. Build connected detail, materials, lighting, spatial background, original motion and integrated UI. Generate and polish the chosen Lux3D assets alongside independent code work. Check permitted camera paths and interaction/reset behavior.
5. Concentrated final polish. Review representative full-resolution frames and actual motion. Correct the highest-impact defects in composition, materials, interaction feedback, lighting and legibility. Perform only focused corrections, not a new concept or unrelated expansion.
6. Deliver and validate. Record the finished scene, inspect beginning/signature/close-up/ending, check video decoding/specifications, produce the PDF and portable sources, and verify the product from its final shared location. Test the actual final build, not just development files.

Do not stop after a plan, scaffolding, attractive still image, asset-generation success or untested export. Continue through the full delivery unless there is a genuine blocker that requires user action. Keep updates concise and evidence-based.
